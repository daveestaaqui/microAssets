## 🧬 Module 1: The Human Microbiome 101

* **Q: What is the human microbiome?**
  * **Answer:** A complex ecosystem of trillions of microorganisms—including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and bacteriophages—living in and on the human body, predominantly within the gastrointestinal tract.
* **Q: Does the microbiome only affect digestion?**
  * **Answer:** No. The microbiome affects systemic human health, including metabolic function, immune system modulation, cardiovascular health, dermatological integrity, and the gut-brain axis (neurotransmitter synthesis).
* **Q: Where is the majority of the body's serotonin produced?**
  * **Answer:** In the gastrointestinal tract by enterochromaffin cells (~90%), deeply influenced by the metabolic activity of gut microbiota.

## 💊 Module 2: Probiotics vs. Prebiotics vs. Synbiotics

* **Q: What is the scientific definition of a Probiotic?**
  * **Answer:** Live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host (as defined by FAO/WHO).
* **Q: What is a Prebiotic?**
  * **Answer:** Non-digestible substrates (like polyphenols and specific fibers) that are selectively utilized by host microorganisms conferring a health benefit.
* **Q: What is a Synbiotic?**
  * **Answer:** A synergistic combination of probiotics and prebiotics that work together to enhance the survival and metabolic activity of beneficial bacteria.
* **Q: Why are most fermented foods (kombucha, kimchi, yogurt) technically not "probiotics"?**
  * **Answer:** While nutritious, they generally do not provide clinically studied strains at specific, verified colony-forming unit (CFU) or active fluorescent unit (AFU) dosages with demonstrated survival through the human GI tract.

* **Q: What is the biggest hurdle for oral probiotics?**
  * **Answer:** Surviving the extreme acidity of the human stomach (pH 1.5–3.5), bile salts, and digestive enzymes in the upper GI tract.
